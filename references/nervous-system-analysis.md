# Nervous-system analysis for clinical reasoning

## Purpose

- **Scope:** For each symptom, integrate relevant neural anatomy, possible lesion/disease processes and mechanical function into system-level candidate explanations.
- **Table distinction:** Table 1.1 identifies specific adjacent and remote neural-source candidates. Table 1.2 explains the relevant pathway, process or functional relationship that makes those candidates clinically meaningful.
- **Stage boundary:** Subjective analysis generates hypotheses from reported symptoms and history. Examination findings are needed to evaluate patient-specific neural function and mechanosensitivity.
- **Selection:** The directions below are prompts for reasoning, not mandatory findings or a disease checklist.

## Step 1: Define the Symptom and Relevant Context

1. **Symptom scope:** Preserve the symptom ID, reported distribution, quality, onset/course and provoking or relieving conditions.
2. **Descriptions:** Clarify ambiguous descriptions such as “numbness”: distinguish reported tingling, reduced sensation, painful abnormal sensation and other meanings without treating self-report as an examination finding.
3. **Source candidates:** Review relevant Table 1.1 candidates, including both adjacent and remote neural structures.
4. **Missing context:** Identify missing details that could change anatomical localization or the proposed process.

## Step 2: Select Relevant Explanatory Directions

### Neural Pathways and Interfaces

- Trace the pathway relevant to the symptom between peripheral branches, parent nerves, plexus, spinal nerves/roots and central structures only as clinically justified.
- Consider candidate sites of compression, irritation or restricted neural movement and the surrounding tissue interfaces.
- State why each selected site or relationship is relevant to this symptom.
- Anatomical continuity permits consideration of remote mechanical relationships; it does not establish abnormality throughout the pathway.
- Multiple candidate sites do not establish simultaneous lesions, double crush or a causal interaction.

### Lesion and Disease Processes

- Consider focal peripheral nerve, plexus, nerve-root or central nervous-system processes when the distribution and history justify them.
- Consider broader disorders, such as polyneuropathy, when supported by distribution, associated symptoms or relevant health history.
- Do not restrict neural disease explanations to compression.
- Distinguish a possible lesion/disease from an established diagnosis.
- Assess whether a lesion/disease could explain the pain separately under the shared [pain-mechanism guide](pain-mechanisms.md). Sensory symptoms without pain are not themselves neuropathic pain.

### Neural Mechanical Function

- Consider whether reported symptom behavior warrants a hypothesis involving neural loading, movement relative to surrounding tissues or increased neural mechanosensitivity.
- Mechanical load, excursion, strain and mechanosensitivity describe different properties; do not use them interchangeably.
- When examining mechanosensitivity, follow the shared pain-mechanism guide for familiar-symptom reproduction, structural differentiation, comparisons and interpretation limits.
- Mechanical responses may inform a functional hypothesis without establishing a particular lesion or neuropathic pain classification.

### Pain Processing and Modulation

- Consider altered pain processing as a possible contribution when relevant history and findings support further assessment.
- Do not infer a specific central mechanism solely from pain duration, severity, widespread distribution or an incomplete local tissue explanation.
- Keep nociplastic pain and central sensitization distinct, following the shared pain-mechanism guide.

## Step 3: State the Candidate and Its Basis

For each selected explanation, record:

1. **Candidate:** The proposed neural process or functional problem.
2. **Case basis:** The reported features that make it worth considering.
3. **Pathway and regions:** Relevant structures, interfaces and anatomical regions, with the proposed connection to the symptom.
4. **Uncertainty:** Missing or inconsistent information and the questions or examination findings that could change the explanation.

Do not require a conclusion about whether multiple candidates are alternatives, coexist or influence one another during subjective Phase 1.

## Step 4: Return to Table 1.2

Preserve the existing three columns:

- **Integrated functions or Systems:** Retain the Nervous system row label.
- **Upstream and Downstream structure:** Record the selected explanation, relevant neural structures/interfaces and a concise case basis. Do not force a single upstream-to-downstream causal chain.
- **Regions This System Passes Through:** Record the regions relevant to that explanation and their proposed relationship. Do not list every region normally traversed by the nervous system.

Use existing reasoning sections for material follow-up questions, verification directions and limitations when they would overcrowd the table. Missing information remains unknown.

## Interpretation Limits

- A remote movement changing symptoms can inform a neural-mechanical hypothesis, but does not by itself locate a lesion.
- Neural function and mechanosensitivity require separate assessment.
- Address severe or progressive neurological deficits and other potentially urgent findings under the [clinical contract](clinical-contract.md) without waiting for table completion.

## Sources and Limits

- [IASP clinical identification guide, 2026](https://www.iasp-pain.org/resources/fact-sheets/identifying-neuropathic-pain-in-the-clinic-a-guide-for-clinicians/): clinical assessment of neuropathic pain; not a universal neural-source localization protocol.
- [Schmid et al., 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2653029/): assessment reliability in 31 people with unilateral arm and/or neck pain and the distinction between neural function and mechanosensitivity; does not establish universal diagnostic thresholds.
- The shared [pain-mechanism guide](pain-mechanisms.md) contains the classification frameworks, supporting sources and limits for neuropathic pain, nociplastic pain and mechanosensitivity reporting.

The table responsibilities and workflow above are project reasoning guidance, not a validated diagnostic algorithm. Sources reviewed 2026-10-29.
