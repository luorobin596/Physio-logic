# Pain descriptors and reasoning limits

Reviewed adaptation of the exported pain mechanism document, 2026-10-27. Historical discussion catalogs, when supplied, preserve candidate ideas rather than diagnostic rules.

- Nociceptive pain: use this descriptor only with a case-supported hypothesis involving non-neural tissue nociception, while noting uncertainty.
- Neuropathic pain: consider somatosensory lesion/disease evidence and an appropriate clinical framework; a nerve-related symptom, mechanosensitivity or isolated test is insufficient to declare the descriptor confirmed.
- Nociplastic pain: keep distinct from central sensitization. Do not assign it solely from duration, stress or a diagnostic name. When discussing formal musculoskeletal criteria, check version, population, relevant findings and missing requirements. Do not transfer them automatically to visceral presentations.
- Mixed contributions may be discussed with separate evidence; these descriptors are not mutually exclusive diagnostic boxes.
- Contributing factors is OPEN: physical, loading, health, environmental, contextual, recovery, psychosocial and other supported factors may matter. Do not force them into a preset taxonomy or equate an observation with causation.

Use possible contributions as hypotheses when the evidence permits, not as guaranteed mechanisms. The package's stars are case-support statuses, not formal possible/probable pain-classification grades.

Sources: [IASP Terminology](https://www.iasp-pain.org/resources/terminology/), [Kosek 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33974577/), [Finnerup 2016](https://doi.org/10.1097/j.pain.0000000000000492). Full checks and source limitations are recorded in the development review; they do not establish every catalog example's validity.
