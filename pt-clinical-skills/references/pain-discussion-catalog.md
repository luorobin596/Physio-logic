# Historical discussion catalog — qualified adaptation

Read only for relevant candidate ideas. The lists below come from pain mechanism.pdf; they are not validated subtypes, diagnostic rules or default case findings. Disease labels do not automatically determine a mechanism. Apply the [pain-mechanism guide](pain-mechanisms.md) and [clinical contract](clinical-contract.md) first. Original page numbers are preserved as source locators; original examples are archived separately. Formal criteria and each method's evidence must be checked before using them. The nociplastic heading/definition has been corrected under R01; other historical list entries remain candidates with unverified detail.

## Source page 1

1) Nociceptive pain
Definition: Pain arising from activation of peripheral nociceptors in non-neural tissue
(tendon, joint, ligament, periosteum, viscera, skin).
Sub-items (choose one or more):
 Mechanical/load-related articular: capsule/ligament strain, synovial
irritation, meniscus/labrum, zygapophyseal/SIJ, disc outer annulus.
 Tendinous/peritendinous: reactive tendinopathy, degenerative
tendinopathy, paratenon irritation.
 Myofascial: active/latent trigger point(s), taut band, predictable referred
patterns.
 Inflammatory/chemical: acute post-injury inflammation, crystal arthropathy,
autoimmune arthritis, post-op inflammatory phase.
 Bone/periosteal: stress reaction, stress fracture, bone marrow edema,
periostitis (e.g., MTSS).
 Ischemic/vascular: exertional compartment-like pain, claudication-type
symptoms, sustained-posture ischemia.
 Cutaneous: superficial nociceptor sensitization (abrasion, dermatitis).
 Visceral/referred: GI/hepatobiliary/renal/cardiac sources with
viscerosomatic referral.
2) Neuropathic pain
Definition: Pain caused by a lesion or disease of the somatosensory nervous system
(peripheral or central).
Sub-items (peripheral):
 Radicular/radiculopathy (nerve root) with dermatomal pain ± neuro deficits.
 Nerve trunk entrapment/compression (e.g., median at carpal tunnel, ulnar
at cubital tunnel, peroneal at fibular neck).
 Plexopathy (brachial, lumbosacral).
 Mononeuropathy multiplex / focal neuropathies.
 Polyneuropathy (diabetic, chemo-induced).
 Small-fiber neuropathy (burning pain, allodynia, dysautonomia).
 Post-herpetic neuralgia.

3) Nociplastic pain
Definition: Consult the reviewed pain-mechanisms guide and IASP definition; do not treat this descriptor as synonymous with central sensitization.
Sub-items (by clinical pattern):
 Regional nociplastic (single region with disproportionate pain, sensory gain,
weak mechanical coupling).
 Widespread central sensitivity syndrome (CSS/fibromyalgia spectrum).
 Chronic primary musculoskeletal pain (ICD-11; e.g., chronic primary low
back/neck pain).
 Headache/orofacial with central sensitization (chronic migraine, tension-
type with allodynia, TMD with widespread hyperalgesia).
 Viscero-nociplastic clusters (IBS, interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome,
chronic pelvic pain, vulvodynia).
 Persistent post-trauma/whiplash-associated disorder (chronic phase) with
central features.
Useful phenotypes (pick if helpful):
 Sensory amplification predominant (temporal summation ↑,
allodynia/hyperalgesia).
 Descending inhibition deficit (impaired conditioned pain modulation, CPM).

 Autonomic dysregulation predominant (sleep disturbance, orthostatic

4) Mixed pain (common combinations)
 Nociceptive + Neuropathic: e.g., lumbar HNP with radicular pain plus
facet/annular nociception.
 Nociceptive + Nociplastic: e.g., knee OA with widespread hyperalgesia and
poor CPM.
 Neuropathic + Nociplastic: e.g., chronic radiculopathy/failed back with
central amplification.
 Tri-mechanism mix: chronic post-surgical shoulder pain with capsular
nociception, suprascapular neuropathy, and central features.
5) Cross-cutting modulators (not
mechanisms, but document as “drivers”)
 Inflammatory load (local heat/swelling, morning stiffness, systemic markers).
 Psychosocial (catastrophizing, fear-avoidance, low self-efficacy, mood/sleep
problems, stress, social isolation).
 Autonomic nervous system (ANS) dysregulation (sympathetic
predominance, vasomotor changes, sweating, HRV↓, orthostatic symptoms).
 Endocrine/metabolic & sleep (short sleep, circadian disruption, thyroid/sex
hormone issues, deconditioning, low physical activity).
 Contextual factors (work demands, family stressors, health literacy).
